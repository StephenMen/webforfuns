The History

Ghost Mountain

Phnum Khmoch, known as "Ghost Mountain," sits along the Thai-Cambodian border — a place where history and horror converge. During the Khmer Rouge regime (1975-1979), this remote region became a site of systematic violence.

The mountain's dense jungles concealed mass graves, execution sites, and forced labor camps. Thousands were brought here from across the country, their fates sealed by a regime that sought to erase an entire generation.

Today, the land still yields bones during the rainy season. The trees grow from burial sites. The earth remembers what the world tried to forget.

Film Synopsis

Blood Border

In the shadow of the Thai-Cambodian border lies Phnum Khmoch — Ghost Mountain — where the earth still holds secrets buried for decades. Blood Border is a harrowing documentary that unearths the untold story of a second killing field, hidden from the world's gaze.

Through intimate survivor testimonies, archaeological discoveries, and haunting archival footage, the film follows investigators as they excavate mass graves and piece together evidence of systematic atrocities committed during the final days of the Khmer Rouge regime.

As bones emerge from the jungle floor and witnesses break decades of silence, the documentary reveals not just a historical tragedy, but an ongoing quest for justice that continues to shape Cambodia's fragile present.

A film about memory, evidence, and the weight of what the world chose to forget.
